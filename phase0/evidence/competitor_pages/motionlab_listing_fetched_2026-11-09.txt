in Animation

Turn Blender motion into data you can understand
MotionLab records evaluated object motion from Blender animations and Rigid Body simulations, calculates useful motion and energy measurements, and presents the results through interactive graphs.
Instead of judging motion only by eye, you can inspect how an object moves over time, compare multiple objects, locate important moments, jump directly to specific frames, and export structured results for further analysis.
Seven motion and energy measurements
Speed — inspect how quickly an object moves
Acceleration — see how velocity changes over time
Angular Velocity — analyze rotational motion
Momentum — examine mass-scaled linear motion
Kinetic Energy — measure energy associated with motion
Potential Energy — inspect gravitational potential estimates
Total Energy — view kinetic and potential energy together
Interactive graph workflow
Zoom and pan through recorded measurements
Switch the horizontal axis between Frames and Seconds
Click a graph to jump directly to the corresponding Blender frame
Display multiple graph series and graph widgets
Assign graph series to the left or right Y axis
Adjust graph colors, order, and visible ranges
Compare the same measurement across multiple objects
Overlay kinetic, potential, and total energy
Events and important frames
MotionLab creates automatic Events for characteristic measurement points. You can also add manual Event markers, filter Events by object or measurement, control their visibility, and use them to navigate the Blender timeline.
Reusable analytical Sessions
Record one or more objects as a MotionLab Session and return to the analytical data later. Loading a Session restores its measurements and Events for review without recreating or animating the original Blender objects.
Structured data export
Completed Sessions can include:
Session manifest and Scene metadata
Recorded object metadata
Rigid Body configuration snapshots
Frame-by-frame transform samples
Derived motion and energy measurements
Automatic and manual Events
Session summary data
CSV and JSON files for spreadsheets, scripts, and external analysis workflows
Animation and physics workflows
MotionLab works with keyframed animation and Rigid Body simulations. Physics Preflight reports potential cache or frame-coverage problems without changing your Scene. Configurable Physics Pre-roll allows a simulation to warm up before recording begins.
Typical uses
Technical animation review
Rigid Body simulation inspection
Motion studies and visualization
Physics education and demonstrations
Comparing the motion of multiple objects
Finding speed, acceleration, and energy peaks
Exporting motion data for additional analysis
Quick start
Select one or more animated or simulated objects.
Set the Blender Scene start and end frames.
Open Scene Properties > MotionLab Session.
Enter a Session name and select an export folder.
Run Physics Preflight when working with a Rigid Body simulation.
Press Start Session and let playback reach the final frame.
Open the MotionLab tab in the 3D View sidebar.
Select a recorded object and measurement, then press Show.
Requirements
Blender 4.5 or later
Windows x64
Installation
Download the supplied MotionLab ZIP file.
In Blender, open Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions.
Open the extension menu and choose Install from Disk.
Select the MotionLab ZIP file.
Enable MotionLab.
Open Scene Properties and the MotionLab tab in the 3D View sidebar.
Privacy and connectivity
MotionLab performs its recording and analysis locally in Blender. No account, license server, or internet connection is required for normal product operation.
Important Session behavior
A loaded MotionLab Session restores analytical data for review. It does not recreate, reposition, or animate the original Blender objects. Mass-dependent measurements use the object and Rigid Body metadata captured during recording.
